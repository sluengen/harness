### Changed — the engine timeout ceiling is 900s (#375)

`engine_timeout_seconds` moves 720 → 900, with `DEFAULT_ENGINE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`
in lockstep, so a repo carrying no `loop:` block of its own runs the new ceiling
too. `probe_budget_seconds` stays at 720: it is clamped to at-or-below the
configured engine ceiling, and this retune was bought for the review engine's
own right tail rather than for a stage that adds cost on top of it.

The same argument that took the ceiling 600 → 720: it had drifted inside the
working distribution. Review timeouts went from zero across ~70 reviews to 22 of
82 from 2026-08-04, while *successful* review latency shifted from a max of 587s
to a median of 510s and a max of 708s against the 720s wall — seven of 53
successes finishing within a minute of it. A review landing just past the ceiling
is killed and reported `engine_timeout`, indistinguishable from a hang, so the
rate was measuring saturation.

The tail past 720s is censored and cannot be read off a ledger that killed it, so
this is a headroom retune rather than a fitted one. A review that now finishes
between 720s and 900s confirms saturation; one still reaching 900s is the hang
the ceiling exists to kill.

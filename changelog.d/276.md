### Changed — a seam extraction must also say where the extracted module's tests went (#276)

- `review-discipline`'s **Architecture watchlist** check gains a second clause on the same *seam extraction* branch #273 amended: confirm the extracted module's tests moved with it — into a test module of their own, or stayed for a stated reason. Tests left behind with no reason stated fold into the **same Medium finding** the bullet already grades. No new bullet, no new trigger, no new severity.
- The gap it closes is test structure silently failing to follow production structure. `code-quality` Part B (*Extract on the third strike*) governs production structure only, and fires at build time — when the tests may not have moved yet. The review gate is the one moment both halves are visible at once, and the bullet already holds the changed-file set from its own `git diff --name-only` comparison, so the confirmation costs nothing extra to run.
- The convention existed but was unstated, so adherence was coincidental: three extractions in one window each got their own test module, while a fourth family's four extractions all landed their tests back in a single 2,768-line module until a later ticket split it.
- Bound to the *extraction* outcome, not to every watchlist touch — a deferral carves nothing out, so no tests were orphaned. And it asks for an *answer*, not a move: tests that stay for a stated reason discharge the obligation, because the reviewer is confirming a decision was made rather than enforcing one outcome.
- Four guards pin it, anchored to the bullet through the slicer that owns it rather than a recopy. The measuring tokens (`test`, `stay`) were each confirmed absent from the pre-change bullet, so neither can be green against the unmodified prose; `module`, `reason` and `Medium` are present elsewhere in the bullet and are therefore asserted only inside a sentence scope. The binding test rejects a clause that mentions tests without tying them to the extraction; one pins that the clause adds no second severity; and an anti-vacuity test fails if the slice over-runs into the neighbouring bullet.
- Mechanizing the check is out of scope by design: the module-to-test-module relationship is not derivable without a naming convention this repo has not declared — `close_telemetry.py` is tested by `test_close_terminal_telemetry.py`. Prose at the review gate is the proportionate instrument; declaring a naming convention is a separate, larger decision.
